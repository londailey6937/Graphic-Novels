## Story

### I

[IMAGE p01 | splash | Walt enters the Olympic rainforest. (He came here to die.)]
Walt had chosen the Olympic wilderness the way some men would choose a room with the gas left on. Quietly. Deliberately. Without a note, because there was no one left to read one.

[IMAGE p02 | quad | He approaches the creek.]
Three weeks of walking had worn the intention down to something duller than resolve — not quite doubt, but adjacent to it. He'd stopped counting days. He'd stopped, mostly, thinking in words at all. The forest had a way of doing that to a mind that came to it broken. It didn't console him. It didn't need to. It simply went on being enormous and indifferent, and there was a mercy in that indifference he hadn't expected.

[IMAGE p03 | quad | He spots something unfamiliar.]
He found the creek by sound before he found it by sight — water over stone, a small, ordinary music. He knelt to drink, and that was when he saw the shape half-fallen into a bank of stinging nettle, arms folded wrong beneath a body that was almost, but not quite, a man's.

### II

[IMAGE p04 | splash | A closer look reveals an alien, half-fallen into a bank of stinging nettle.]
Later, Walt would not be able to say how long he sat with it. The skin was grey-green going to ash at the extremities, a color no fever produced in anything born of Earth. Whatever it was, it had gone into the nettle bed either by accident or desperation, and its body had answered the ordinary poison of an ordinary plant the way no ordinary body should: catastrophically. Foam at the corner of a mouth built strangely, like a mouth that had been designed by someone who'd only heard mouths described. A pulse, faint, at a throat with two ridges where a throat should have one.

[IMAGE p05 | quad | Walt examines the alien.]
He didn't run. He would think about that, after — why he didn't run. Maybe a man who has already decided to end his own life has less fear left over for other things.

[IMAGE p07 | quad | He remains with it as night falls.]
He built a fire he didn't need warmth for and sat beside a dying stranger from somewhere that was not anywhere he had a word for, and did the only human thing he had left in him, which was to not leave.

[IMAGE p08 | splash | Its breathing grows shallow. Then, it stops.]
The alien did not wake to thank him. It did not wake at all. Sometime past midnight, by the fire's low red logic, its breathing changed from labored to shallow to something that might have been called peaceful in a human chest, and did not resume.

[IMAGE p06 | quad | He notices a silver band with small studs at its wrist.]
Walt sat with the body until dawn found the silver band around its wrist and made it glint.

### III

[IMAGE p09 | splash | Dawn. The band closes itself on Walt's wrist.]
It should have stayed on the dead. Walt knew this the way you know things about other people's belongings — you don't take them. But when he reached out, not to steal, only to look, the band was already moving. It opened along a seam he hadn't seen and closed itself around his wrist with a small sound like a held breath finally let go.

[IMAGE p10 | quad | He looks at the band, feeling its presence.]
There was no voice. He wants to be clear about that, later, in the only ways he has to be clear about anything — the tightening of a jaw, the specific stillness of a man remembering something he cannot say aloud. There was no voice. There was a pressure, an impression laid directly into the parts of him that language is built on top of, like a hand set flat against a window from the other side of the glass.

[IMAGE p13 | splash | He feels the impression: “Not finished. Not yet finished. Come.”]
Not finished. Not yet finished. Come.

He did not come. Not that day. He sat instead with a body that had chosen, in its last accidental act, to hand him something he had not asked for and could not refuse — and Walt, who had walked into these woods to stop being anything to anyone, found that he had become, against all his intentions, responsible for something again.

### IV

[IMAGE p15 | quad | He looks at the band again, understanding.]
The changes were slow enough, at first, to deny.

[IMAGE p11 | quad | His perception sharpens. He notices things he hadn't before.]
Colors sharpened before he noticed the sharpening. He could hold four threads of a problem in his mind where he used to lose the second one reaching for the third. He needed less. Less food, less sleep, less noise to feel steady — as if some tax he had been paying his whole life without knowing it had simply, quietly, been waived.

[IMAGE p12 | quad | That morning, awake, he checks the band.]
He was afraid of none of this in the way you'd expect. He was afraid the way you're afraid of good news you don't trust — afraid that it would be taken back, that he would wake one morning stripped of it and left only with the knowing of what he'd had.

[IMAGE p14 | quad | The impression calls him onward.]
The band did not speak in words even once. It spoke in urges — a pull, northwest, toward the low grassy meadow where the ground didn't sit quite right, where moss grew in patterns too even to be moss, where the birds had gone quiet in a rough circle nearly forty yards across and never explained why.

[IMAGE p16 | quad | He sets out, walking northwest.]
He went there because something in him that was no longer entirely his own wanted him to, and because — for the first time since he'd left what used to be his home — something wanted him at all.

### V

[IMAGE p17 | splash | He is led to it. Not a hull so much as a held breath in the land itself.]
He found the ship the way you find a face in a cloud: not seeing it, and then unable to stop seeing it. Not a hull so much as a held breath in the land itself, grass grown thick and strange across curves too smooth for hillside, mud settled into it the way mud settles into something that has been patient for a very long time.

[IMAGE p18 | quad | He finds the entrance.]
It did not open for him with drama. It simply, at some point he couldn't mark, had already let him in, the way a held-open door only becomes noticeable once you're standing through it.

Inside, there was no mother waiting to explain herself. There was, instead, a long low gallery, dim and warm as a held hand, and in it: others.

[IMAGE p19 | splash | He sees the kept — none human, none alike, some upright, some coiled, some barely light held in a shape.]
Not human. Not all of them anything he had names for. Beings who had also, somewhere, in some ordinary meadow or reef or cave on some world Walt would never see, been found wanting by whatever standard their own lives had judged them by — and had been picked up anyway. Kept anyway. Not repaired. Not fixed. Kept, the way you keep something you've decided is worth keeping regardless of its condition.

He understood, standing there, without a single word being spoken to him, that this was not a laboratory. It was closer to a house that had never once, in all its ranging through the dark between worlds, turned anyone away at the door.

### VI

[IMAGE p20 | splash | She comes to him the way weather comes — light and pressure filling the room. He never sees a face.]
She came to him the way weather comes — not arriving, exactly, more like a pressure system settling in around him until he realized the air itself had changed. He never saw a face, if she had one to see. What he felt was closer to being known, entirely, all at once, the way you're known by someone who has watched you your whole life without you ever noticing them watching.

[IMAGE p21 | quad | He understands it: hundreds of faint lights, one new and his.]
There was a tone, low and layered, that he came to understand — not through translation, but through recognition — was made of many smaller tones together. Hundreds. Maybe more. Each one a life she had gathered up rather than let end unwitnessed. And somewhere in that chord, faint and new, was a note that was his.

[IMAGE p22 | splash | The specific, unbearable relief of being seen by something that did not require him to have turned out well. He weeps.]
He wept, there, in a way he had not let himself do in the woods with a rope he never used and a plan he never finished. Not from fear. From the specific, unbearable relief of being seen by something that did not require him to have turned out well.

### VII

The agencies came the way agencies come — not with understanding, but with radar returns and thermal anomalies and a very ordinary human fear of anything they could not yet file a report on. Vehicles at the tree line. Voices on channels not meant for civilians. A meadow that had kept its secret for longer than any of their instruments, finally giving it up to men who would only ever see a threat where Walt had found the first mercy of his life.

[IMAGE p23 | splash | He stands at the threshold as the tree line lights with approaching headlights. Unmarked vehicles. Men with rifles and forms. Channels not meant for civilians.]
He stood at the threshold of the ship as the tree line lit with approaching headlights, and understood, with a clarity the bracelet had given him and would now, in its own way, take back, that there was no version of tonight where he walked out to explain himself to men with rifles and forms to fill out. They would not believe the truth. They would not have room, in whatever story they told themselves about tonight, for a homeless man made whole by something that only ever wanted to keep him.

The choice, when it came, did not feel like a choice so much as an admission of something already true.

He looked back once — at the tree line, the lights, the country that had, in its way, already let him go months before any of this began — and then did not look back again.

The ship did not rise in fire or fury. It simply, unhurriedly, was no longer where it had been, the grass springing slowly back over the shape it left behind, the way grass forgets almost everything, given time.

### VIII

[IMAGE p24 | splash | They find nothing. The grass springs slowly back over the shape it left behind.]
Somewhere behind him, men would search a meadow and find nothing but flattened earth and a scorch of confusion they'd spend years trying to close a file on.

Somewhere ahead of him — nowhere, and everywhere, and every meadow and reef and dark corner of every world she had ever quietly loved something out of its ending — Walt's tone settled in among the others.

It was not a triumph. He had not been saved, exactly, or fixed, or made a hero of. He had simply, finally, after a life spent believing himself disposable, been kept.

And somewhere below, in a country that would never know what it had actually lost that night, the forest closed back over the place he'd once gone to disappear — and did, at last, what forests do best.

It kept his secret, too.
